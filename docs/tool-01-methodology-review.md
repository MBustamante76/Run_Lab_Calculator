# Tool 01 methodology review

Status: **approved by RUN-LAB on 15 October 2026.**

This document describes how Tool 01, "Predict my times at other distances", chooses a prediction method. It records the decisions RUN-LAB made at Checkpoint 5A.

Source of truth: `src/calculation/prediction/predictionConfig.ts`. Sample figures below are generated by that code and checked by `tests/prediction/methodologyDocument.test.ts`.

World Athletics points are not used anywhere in Tool 01. They are reserved for Tool 06.

## 1. Strategy-routing flow

The router checks inputs, then tries methods in a fixed order. The first method that applies is used. If none applies, the result is unsupported and contains no time.

1. **Validate.** One or two PBs. Each PB must have a supported distance and a time greater than zero. Two PBs must be at different distances. The target may not match either PB distance. Any failure returns `invalid` with field errors.
2. **Two-PB interpolation.** Used only when two PBs are given, the target lies strictly between them, and the longer anchor is no more than 4 times the shorter one.
3. **Event-pair relationship.** Tried from the nearest PB first. Used only when an approved empirical KsA relationship exists for that PB distance and the target. None are configured yet.
4. **Power-law fallback.** Tried from the nearest PB first. Used only when both distances are 1500m or longer and the longer distance is no more than 4 times the shorter.
5. **Unsupported.** No time is returned.

"Nearest" is measured as a distance ratio, so 1500 m is nearer to 3000 m than 800 m is.

Methods marked `draft` in the configuration are never used in production. They can be switched on only for development review, and then carry the warning "This method is a draft awaiting RUN-LAB approval." Every method in the current configuration is `approved`.

### Outside-anchor rule

When two PBs are given but the target is outside both, the personal two-PB relationship is not extended. The router uses the nearest relevant PB, but only when an approved strategy exists for it (step 3 or 4). Otherwise the result is unsupported.

When a single PB is used this way, the result carries this warning:

> Your target is outside your two PB distances, so your two-PB relationship was not extended. This estimate uses your {distance} PB only.

## 3. Approved and unapproved routes

| Route | Status | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Two PBs bracketing the target, anchor ratio ≤ 4 | Approved | Personal interpolation |
| 1500m to 5000m and 10K, both directions, ratio ≤ 4 | Approved | Power-law fallback, 1500m and longer only |
| 1500m, 1 Mile and 2000m to 10K | Unsupported | Distance ratio above 4 |
| Any single PB from 100m to 1000m | Unsupported | No approved KsA relationships configured; outside the power-law range |
| 800m as a power-law source or target | Unsupported | Excluded from the power-law fallback |
| 100m or 200m to 5000m or 10K | Blocked in code | A sprint PB never routes to a long-distance target, whatever the configuration |

## 4. Configured methods

### Two-PB interpolation

- User-facing label: "Based on your two PBs"
- Version: `1.0.0`
- Maximum anchor ratio: **4** (longer anchor distance ÷ shorter anchor distance)
- Formula: \(k = \ln(T_2/T_1) / \ln(D_2/D_1)\), then \(T = T_1 \times (D/D_1)^k\)
- The curve passes through both PBs exactly. Swapping the PBs does not change the result.

### Event-pair relationships

- **None configured.** Only approved empirical KsA relationships may be added. Sex-neutral short-distance coefficients are not invented for V1.
- The structure supports: source event, target event, model ID, version, a multiplier or exponent, direction (one way or both), evidence note, and enabled flag.

### Power-law fallback

- User-facing label: "Middle and long-distance scaling"
- Version: `1.06-1.0.0`
- Exponent: **1.06**. A configurable fallback for 1500m and longer only, not a universal model.
- Maximum distance ratio: **4**
- Permitted PB distances: 1500m, 1 Mile, 2000m, 3000m, 5000m, 10K
- Permitted targets: 1500m, 1 Mile, 2000m, 3000m, 5000m, 10K
- Formula: \(T_2 = T_1 \times (D_2/D_1)^{1.06}\)

## 5. Maximum permitted ratios and spans

| Limit | Value | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Two-PB anchor ratio | 4 | 200m + 800m (ratio 4) can interpolate; 200m + 1000m (ratio 5) cannot |
| Power-law distance ratio | 4 | 1500m → 5000m (3.33) allowed; 1500m → 10K (6.67) not allowed |
| Power-law distances | 1500m and longer | 800m and shorter never use the power law |
| Sprint source to long target | Always blocked | 100m, 200m or 400m to 3000m, 5000m or 10K never predicts from that PB |

## 7. Labels and warning copy

- Every successful result is labelled: **Estimated equivalent performance**
- Interpolation explanation: "Estimated between your 200m PB of 25.0 and your 800m PB of 2:10.0. The 400m lies between those two distances."
- Power-law explanation: "Estimated from your 1500m PB of 4:30.0, scaled to 3000m."
- Power-law assumption: "This estimate assumes a similar level of endurance across both distances."
- Outside anchors: see the outside-anchor rule above.
- Unsupported: "We need a more relevant PB to estimate this distance reliably. Add a second PB closer to the target distance, or choose a nearer event."

## 8. Model and version visibility

Users see the friendly method label, for example "Middle and long-distance scaling". The exact method ID and version, for example `power-law` and `1.06-1.0.0`, stay internal. They are carried on every result for diagnostics and tests, but are not shown in the Tool 01 interface.
